Why this course exists

Most "AI engineering" courses are tool surveys. This one is not. By the end of the fourteen days you will have read vLLM's scheduler line-by-line and shipped a patch upstream, implemented PagedAttention in Triton and beaten the reference kernel in at least one regime, hand-rolled a KV-quantisation scheme from the KIVI paper, written a Pallas flash-attention kernel that wins against cuDNN in your benchmark, distributed-trained a 1.3B model in JAX, and built three agentic systems that operate on a real data platform (lineage-aware dbt generation, CDC-driven schema migration, plan-aware SQL optimisation). The capstone composes the inference work and the data-platform work into a multi-tenant system you can defend on cost.

The course assumes you already know the basics. There is no chapter on "what is a transformer." There is a chapter on why BlockManagerV2 exists, what its eviction policy gets wrong on long-lived chat sessions, and how to patch it.

Prerequisites

  • Comfortable reading C++/CUDA. You will read flash-attention and the vLLM CSRC tree.
  • Have written a Pallas or Triton kernel before, even a small one. Day 8 assumes this.
  • Have shipped an inference system. You know what nvidia-smi shows you and you have written a load test.
  • Familiar with JAX's sharding model (shard_map, meshes, axis names). Day 7 starts at the deep end.
  • Familiar with at least one columnar warehouse (BigQuery, Snowflake, ClickHouse, DuckDB) and one CDC pipeline (Debezium, Materialize, Estuary).
  • Hardware access: at least one H100 for the inference labs; ideally 8x H100 for Day 9. A100 works with caveats noted in each lab.
