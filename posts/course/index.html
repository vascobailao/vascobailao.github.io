Why this course

The official certification material for AI coding tools is, with rare exceptions, vendor marketing dressed as education. It tells you what features the vendor's product has. It does not tell you when those features actually help, when they get in the way, how the tools compare, or how to measure whether you are getting value from any of them. After teaching engineers to use these tools for years, I am convinced the gap between "I have a Copilot license" and "I am genuinely faster because of AI" is mostly a matter of unlearning the marketing and learning the engineering.

This course is the version I wish existed. It covers the same surface area as the major certifications (responsible use, prompt crafting, code generation, refactoring, testing, debugging) but it does so across seven tools instead of one, with real labs you can run on your own machine, and with an honest treatment of where each tool wins and loses. By the end of it you will have a personal evaluation rubric, a productivity baseline, and a clear answer to the question "which of these should I actually use, and for what."

The cadence is one lesson per day for fourteen days. Each lesson takes 60 to 120 minutes including the labs. You can compress the schedule if you want; you cannot skip the labs and learn anything useful.

How to use this course: read the lesson, run every lab, write down what surprised you. The labs are the load-bearing piece. Skipping them and reading the prose will give you opinions to repeat at parties; running them will give you taste.

What you will need: a laptop with at least 16GB RAM, accounts on the relevant tools (most have free tiers), a small open-source repo to experiment in, and the discipline to actually measure things rather than vibe-check them.

Cadence: one lesson published per day at this URL. Bookmark it. New days appear at the top of the grid as they ship.
